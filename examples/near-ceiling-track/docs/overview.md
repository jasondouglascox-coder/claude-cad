# Near-ceiling HO track: design overview

A 3D-printed bridge shelf that carries a single HO track around a room, 12" below the ceiling. Everything is
generated by the `hotrain` package. The design decisions and why are in `docs/specs/*.md`; each spec ends with
Amendments that record every change of mind. Wall measurements are in `docs/measurements.md`.

## The layout

- Vintage **DC** HO trains (not DCC), **Code 100** nickel-silver rail (kept over Code 83 for deep vintage flanges),
  single track. Rail top 12" below a 108" ceiling.
- The track runs in a closed loop along the walls of an office, 1.75" out from the wall:
  1. Down the long right wall (170"), past a double French door near the back.
  2. A **bridge corner** across the mouth of a 43.75" alcove onto a bump-out.
  3. Along the 82.5" back wall.
  4. An **inside corner** at the back-left, then down the left wall.
  5. An inside corner at the front-left, then along the front wall.
  6. An inside corner at the front-right, back onto the right wall.

  `hotrain/room.py` models the whole office: teal walls, white doors. The front wall is left out of the render.
- The room isn't square: the front wall is 3/8" shorter than the back wall plus the alcove. The model is drawn
  square, and the straight runs take up the difference (see `docs/measurements.md`).

## Straight runs

Each **bay** runs between two wall brackets, 8"–18.9" apart (each half span must fit the 256 mm bed). Bays are
set by `BAYS` in `hotrain/layout.py`. Two looks share every interface (`run_style`):

- **`deck_truss`** (the chosen look): a Warren deck truss 2.25" deep hanging between 3" concrete-look piers (cap
  ledge, flared sides). It has inclined end posts, uprights every 2", and a small bow-tie key in a bottom-chord
  splice plate at mid-bay.
- **`arch`** (the code default): open-spandrel arches split at the crown with a bow-tie keystone.

Common to both:

- **Panels:** two per bay, one under each rail, each printed as two halves.
- **Cross-frames:** at each post. They hold the panel spacing, carry X sway bracing on the deck truss, and carry a
  wall-side finger that cradles the bus wires.
- **Wall brackets:** an L-flange with vertical slots for #8 screws (±¼" leveling) into studs or self-drilling
  drywall anchors. The brackets are **half-lapped** with the panels: the panels' pier notch goes up half the pier and
  the bracket slots come down the other half. A snap latch on each bracket clicks into a window in the pier.
- **Tie ladders:** open ties, see-through from below, on two strips under the rails, with gauge-setting tie plates.
  Spike lips sit on the outside of the rail only, so deep flanges clear. Jigsaw joints sit at the quarter points.
  The ladders print in **two colors with one change**: brown up to the tie tops, gray tie plates above.
- **Solder gap:** at each bay's mid, the plain ties between two handrail posts are left out beside the rails. That
  gives an iron access to the **outside** of each rail.
  - A **feeder groove** runs straight across under both rails in the gap's first opening, next to the full tie. The
    rest of the gap is clear for bending the wire and soldering. A 0.8 mm floor under the groove keeps the ladder
    in one piece.
  - The wall rail's feeder drops behind the wall-side panel.
  - The room rail's feeder ducks under both rails through the groove and drops on the wall side too, to the bus.
  - On the room side, fake tie ends keep the tie line unbroken.
- **Handrail:** on the room side. It's also the derail catch: a test tips a 60 ft car and requires the railing to
  stop it within 25°. On deck truss runs it's a short single-bar walkway railing.

## Corners

- One radius everywhere: R = 550.3 mm (21.67"). Spans are 172.2 mm, one per 18° of turn. Curved spans are straight
  chords, and the track curves through them.
- The inside corner has 7 spans; the bridge corner has 9.
- **Structure:** two ¼" aluminum rods bent to the curve. Floor beams cradle the rods; wall anchors and 6" deep
  brackets hold the floor beams and rod ends. The printed bending jig is a **gauge**: bend a little past it, since
  6061 springs back.
- **Deck:** one flat printable ladder per span (brown ties, gray tie plates on top). It rests on the two rods,
  which sit in shallow grooves under the ties.
- **Side frames:** Pratt **pony trusses** (`corner_style="pony"`; `"bowstring"` also builds).
  - Ledges on the inside of each truss's bottom form a U that hugs the deck's tie ends (glued).
  - The outside reads as solid gray.
  - A step at each end sits the truss on the floor beam.
- **Bridge corner drop:** 50 lb **braided** line (not monofilament, which creeps) around tie ends outside a truss,
  up to a small **horn cleat** screwed into a ceiling joist. The cleat can be re-tied with its screws in.
- The bus wires run in hooks under the floor beams, on the wall side, below the deep brackets' arms.

## Printing

- Bambu A1, PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, **no supports**. Every Z level sits on a 0.2 mm multiple.
- **Two filaments:** brown and gray. The ceiling cleats are white PLA.
- **Plates open colored in Bambu Studio.** Each 3MF is a Bambu project (`cadkit.bambu3mf`) with a filament per
  part: 1 gray PETG, 2 brown PETG, 3 white PLA (`filament_*` in params). A plain 3MF would load as geometry only,
  all on filament 1.
- **At most one color change per plate:**
  - Two-color parts (tie ladders, corner decks) are pooled onto their own plates.
  - Each two-color part is one object with a brown "wood" part and a gray "steel" part.
  - Every other plate is single-color.
- **Gray only over brown:** a tie plate never hangs over a hole. A tie over a panel tab's slot gets no plate, so
  the slot is open to the sky.
- **Print the fit-test coupon first.** It has 3 variants each of the tie-plate groove, tab slot, keystone socket,
  latch and rod-groove seat. Pick the one that fits, then retune `fit_slide`, `fit_snap` and `rail_base_w` in
  `hotrain/params.py`.

## Wiring

- DC power pack (≥1 A, no pulse power).
- 14–16 AWG bus along the wall: in the cross-frame cradles on straights, in the floor-beam hooks on corners.
- 20–22 AWG feeders soldered to the outside of the rail at the bay solder gaps. No feeders on corners.
- Feeders join the bus with lever connectors (e.g. WAGO 221), so no soldering on the ceiling.
